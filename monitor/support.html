Rainbow's End Monitor — Support

The free crew companion to Rainbow's End Anchoring. Answers in the order the questions come up. Anything not covered: rainbow@svrainbow.net.

Getting started

What does the Monitor do, and what do I need?

It lets crew watch a boat's anchor watch and find their way back to her. The boat runs Rainbow's End Anchoring (the paid app); when the owner arms the anchor watch and switches on Share with crew, the boat shows an eight-character crew code. Type that code into the Monitor and you see what the boat sees — the fence, the boat, her trail — with an alarm on your phone if she breaks out or falls silent, and an arrow that points at her. You need an iPhone or iPad and the code. No Apple ID, account, or sign-in is needed on your phone. Without a code, the app can show you only its demo.

Can I try it without a boat?

Yes. Tap Open the demo watch on the first screen, or type the code DEMO-DEMO. It is a boat called Rainbow lying to her anchor, built into the app with no network needed. "Find the boat" in the demo places her 600 ft northeast of wherever you are standing, so the arrow has somewhere real to point and the figures change as you walk.

Do I need to be on the same Apple ID as the boat?

No. That is the whole point of the code. The boat publishes one small record, encrypted with a key made from the code, to the public part of the app's own iCloud storage; your phone computes the same key and opens it. Anyone with the code can read the watch; nobody without it can, and nobody but the boat can change or delete it.

Joining

It says "No watch under that code."

Either the boat is not sharing right now — the watch was disarmed, or "Share with crew" is off — or a character is wrong. Codes are two groups of four from an alphabet with no O, I, 0 or 1, so an O is always a zero-shaped letter that does not exist: try 0 instead of O and 1 instead of I. Ask the boat to read it out again if in doubt.

It says "That watch ended."

The boat's last report is more than a day old. That usually means a phone aboard died with sharing on, or nobody took the watch down. The app treats a record older than a day as a watch that ended rather than as a boat sitting quietly in her fence. Ask the boat to arm again.

Do I have to type the code every day?

No. Boats this phone has joined are listed on the first screen; tap one to look for her again. The boat's code stays the same until the owner chooses a new one, so a crew who joined at the dock on Monday still find her on Friday. "Forget this boat" removes an entry.

The watch

What do the alarms mean?

There are two. A breach: the boat reports she is outside her fence; your phone sounds within seconds of the boat's report. Silence: the boat reports about every 20 seconds; if nothing arrives for a minute and a half the screen says the link is going stale, and after three minutes your phone sounds, because a dead link must never look like a safe boat. Silence usually means the boat lost her signal or the phone aboard slept; it is telling you to find out, not that she has dragged. Every age is measured by iCloud's clock, never the boat's.

Can I silence or stop the alarm on the boat from here?

No. Silence here quiets your phone only; the boat keeps its own watch and only the boat can disarm it. Crew can watch and find; they cannot change anything aboard. That is deliberate: a lost phone ashore must never become an unwatched boat.

The screen says "The boat took the watch down."

The owner disarmed the watch or switched sharing off, and the record was deleted. Nothing is being reported now. If the boat arms again with the same code, look for her from the first screen. "Find the boat" still works from her last known position.

Finding the boat

How does the arrow work?

It uses your phone's GPS and compass and the boat's last position. Hold the phone flat, top edge forward, and turn until the arrow points up; the line beneath reads the bearing and the distance, in feet or meters close in and nautical miles farther out. While the boat's reports are live the arrow follows her latest fix. If the boat reports an alarm it keeps following her latest fix, because a dragging boat is not at her anchor. If the reports have gone stale with no alarm, it points at the anchor — the one thing that does not move.

The arrow is grey, or says to move the phone in a figure eight.

The compass is not yet trusted. Move the phone in a figure eight for a few seconds, away from steel and magnets — a winch, a stanchion, a phone case with a magnet. The bearing and distance figures still print while the arrow is grey, because they need no compass.

It says "You're alongside — look up."

You are within about fifty feet. GPS error is larger than the distance by then, and a spinning arrow at the boarding ladder would be worse than none. Look up.

I have no signal on the dock. Does anything work?

Yes. The phone keeps the boat's last known position under each code. With no signal the arrow points at where she was and says how long ago; the first screen offers Find her from her last known position even after the app has been closed. A boat at anchor does not move, so this is the ordinary state on most dinghy docks, not an error.

What does the Map toggle do?

It draws the same fence, boat, anchor and trail on Apple Maps, with you on it if location is allowed. It is off by default because the plot and the arrow need no signal and the map's tiles do; with no signal you still see the dots on a blank grid. It is a picture, not a chart — no depths, no moorings.

The fine print

Does it need my location, and where does it go?

Location is asked for only when you open "Find the boat" or switch the map on, and only while the app is in use. It is used on your phone to point the arrow and draw your dot, and it goes nowhere: the boat never learns where you are, and we never see it. The app collects nothing and never writes to iCloud. See the privacy policy.

Is it free? Are there purchases?

Free, no purchases, no accounts. The boat side — the anchor watch itself — is part of Rainbow's End Anchoring, which the boat's owner buys once.
