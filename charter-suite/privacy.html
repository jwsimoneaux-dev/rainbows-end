Privacy Policy

Charter Suite · Effective September 22, 2026

The short version: the Charter Suite is four Rainbow’s End apps sold together, and buying them together changes nothing about your privacy. None of the four sends anything to us — ever. None has an account, a server of ours, or any tracking.

What the suite is

The suite is a way of buying, not an app of its own. It installs four apps — Rainbow’s End Anchoring, Provisioning, Ship’s Purse, and Logbook — and each keeps what you enter in its own storage on your device, under its own privacy policy, linked below. The four do not share data with one another, and the suite adds nothing to what any of them does.

Buying the suite

The purchase is handled entirely by the App Store, which also credits any of the apps you already own. We receive no payment details, no card numbers, and no personal information from your purchase.

What can leave your device, app by app

Each app keeps what you enter on your device. Here is everything that can ever leave it, and in every case only when you choose.

Anchoring. Your GPS position, only while an anchor watch is armed, and used only on the device — with “always” permission if you allow it, so the watch keeps running with the phone locked. The watch leaves the device only if you share it: to the private database of your own iCloud, for your other devices; or, encrypted under a code you hand to your crew, to the app’s public iCloud database, with the boat’s name, hull color, and photo if you have added one. With the optional satellite map switched on, Apple Maps receives the area shown in order to draw it. Anchoring’s privacy policy

Provisioning. Nothing, unless you print or export a calendar or grocery list yourself, or switch on iCloud sync, which keeps your voyages in your own iCloud. The food allergies you record stay with the rest of your data, and print on the calendar you hand the crew, because that is what they are for. Provisioning’s privacy policy

Ship’s Purse. Nothing, until you switch sharing on or export something yourself. The purse — the people, the rounds, the ledger and any receipt photos — stays on your device. Switch sharing on and it is encrypted under an eight-character code you hand to the crew and written to the app’s public iCloud database, where nobody without the code can read it, ourselves included; switching sharing off, taking a new code, or deleting the purse removes it. The camera and the photo library are asked for only at the tap that attaches a receipt. A statement, a spreadsheet or a backup goes wherever you send it. Ship’s Purse’s privacy policy

Logbook. Nothing, until you share the PDF yourself. The days, the pictures and the ledger stay on the device. It asks for your location only at the tap of Mark position on a day, reads one position, writes it into that day, and never reads it in the background or sends it anywhere. Logbook’s privacy policy

What none of them does

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party data-collection software, in any of the four. None reads your contacts.

Children

None of the four collects data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

Whenever one of the four apps changes what it does with your data, its own policy is updated first; this page is revised to match, with the effective date above changed and the change described here plainly rather than buried.
