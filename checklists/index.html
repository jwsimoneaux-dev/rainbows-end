Rainbow’s End Boat Orientation

Know any boat before you sail.

The charter base hands over the keys at four, or the owner has left them with the dockmaster. Either way, the fuel shutoff is somewhere you haven’t looked yet. Rainbow’s End Boat Orientation walks you around a boat you don’t know, notes where things are, and then runs her by the list. On your device, bought once, with no account.

In beta on TestFlight · headed for the App Store

Why it stands out

  • The orientation walkFind the gear and controls that matter in an emergency, one line at a time: the fire extinguishers, the fuel shutoff, the seacocks, the bilge pumps, the VHF, the liferaft and more, up to 21, chosen from the boat’s profile. Type a few words of where each one is, or mark it Not aboard.
  • The notes go where you need themFrom then on, the lists and the emergency cards say where things are on this boat. The Fire card reads “On Halcyon: Fuel shutoff: port cockpit locker.” Where is it? shows every note in one place.
  • Chartered, borrowed or on deliveryTell the app how you have the boat, and the lists follow. A delivery adds questions for the owner and a go or no-go before the passage. A charter leaves out what the base takes care of.
  • Fitted to the boatA canoe gets a paddler’s lines: wear the PFD, a whistle on it, a spare paddle. An open boat drops the lines about going below. A diesel never sees a gasoline line. Mark something Not aboard on the walk, and the lines about it go too. For boats up to 65 ft.

What it does

  • Setup firstBefore any list, the app asks how you have the boat, then the boat, then the trip. Not sure what is aboard? The lists keep the line until you know.
  • Twelve listsThe orientation walk, questions for the owner or base, boarding a boat that has been sitting, fit for the passage, pre-departure, engine start, casting off and docking, the passenger and crew briefings, the fuel dock, arrival, and the documents aboard. Each names its sources.
  • Questions for the ownerBefore you arrive, ask what only the owner or the base can tell you: the keys and codes, known faults, and when the engine was last serviced.
  • Fit for the passageBefore a long leg, check the fuel, the steering, the rig and the pumps, run a short sea trial, and decide whether to go.
  • Today’s conditionsTap what today holds: night, offshore, children aboard, heavy weather, cold water. The list adds the lines for it, such as “Dress for a fall into the water, not for the air.”
  • A name on every lineName the crew once for the trip. Whoever ticks a line is recorded beside it, and each finished list is kept as a record. Share it as a PDF or as text.
  • Yours to changeHide a line, reword it, add your own, or put the lines in your own order, and restore the original whenever you like. Write lists of your own, or start from a copy of one of ours.
  • Read aloudThe phone reads the briefings and the emergency cards aloud. The Mayday and Pan-Pan calls are yours to make.
  • The emergency cardsMayday, Pan-Pan, man overboard, fire, flooding, abandon ship, collision, grounding, medical, carbon monoxide, lightning and engine failure, as numbered steps in large type. Each names its source.
  • The Mayday call, filled inThe boat’s name, her description, the persons aboard and, if you allow it, the position from the phone’s GPS.
  • Who to callThe charter company or the owner, by phone or VHF, on the Emergency tab and under the steps that say to call for help.
  • Always in reachA strip on every screen, two actions on the app’s Home Screen icon, and a Lock Screen widget. The night view turns the app red on black.
  • On the iPadThe list and the run side by side.
  • OfflineNo account, no server of ours, no tracking. Nothing you enter leaves your device unless you share a record yourself. The position is read only for the Mayday and Pan-Pan calls, and never stored or sent.

It came from our own sailing, aboard our ketch Rainbow and on boats we chartered, for the first hour aboard a boat you don’t know yet.

What it won’t do

  • Call for help. The Mayday card is the script you read into your VHF; the app sends nothing.
  • Keep maintenance, expiration dates or an inventory. Those are Rainbow’s End Manifest’s, the book of your boat.
  • Put your lists or records on a server of ours. They live on your phone and leave only as the record you share.

Rainbow’s End Boat Orientation is an aid to preparation, not a substitute for the skipper’s judgment. Follow your training and the instructions of the Coast Guard.
