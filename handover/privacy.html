Privacy Policy

Rainbow’s End Handover — The boat’s condition, recorded — Effective October 4, 2026

The short version: Rainbow’s End Handover sends nothing to us. Your handovers, photos, readings, voice memos and signatures stay on your device. They leave it only when you share a report or send an expense yourself, or, if you use crew photographers, as sealed records in iCloud under the handover’s code, which we never see.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you put into Rainbow’s End Handover — the boat’s name and type, the charter company or owner, the base, the dates, the names of the skipper and crew, each area’s photos and the marks on them, the readings, the equipment, the base’s briefing as notes or a voice memo, the signatures, every fault and its history, the returns and their receipts, and the fingerprints of the sealed walks — is stored in the app’s own storage on your device. The app has no server of ours and no account system.

Deleting a handover, or the app, deletes this data from the device. If you back up your device, your backup may include the app’s data like any other app’s; backups are managed by Apple under Apple’s terms, and we never see them.

The camera

Walk-through photos come only from the app’s own camera. The app asks for the camera, through the system’s own prompt, the first time you open it. Each photo is kept in the app’s own folder on the device, never in your photo library, and its fingerprint is worked out on the device.

Position

Each walk-through photo records where it was taken. The app asks for your location while in use, through the system’s own prompt, the first time you open the camera. It reads your position when the camera opens and as you take photos, and writes it beside each photo. It never reads your location in the background, and it goes nowhere except inside a report you share or a crew photo, as described below.

The microphone

The base’s briefing can be recorded as a voice memo. The app asks for the microphone only when you tap Record, and only after you have confirmed that everyone being recorded agrees. The recording stays on the device.

The photo library

The photo library is used only to add a receipt that reached you as a picture. The app asks at that tap. The picture you choose is copied into the app and marked as imported; your library is not changed.

Reports

The Condition at delivery and Final turnover reports are PDFs made on your device and handed to the system’s share sheet only when you tap to share one. Where a report goes from there (Mail, AirDrop, Messages, Files, a printer) is your choice, made each time. We never see it.

Crew photographers

If you choose to share a walk with crew photographers, the app uses Apple’s iCloud to carry the work between phones. Nothing goes to iCloud until you tap Start sharing.

What travels: from your phone, the handover’s name, the boat’s name, the base, the names of the skipper and crew, the areas and who has each, which photos have arrived, and at checkout the check-in photos of the areas the crew hold, so their cameras can line up with them. From each crew phone, that person’s photos with the time, position and caption of each.

How it travels: each record is sealed (AES-256-GCM) under a key made on the phone from the handover’s eight-character code, and stored in the public database of our iCloud container under a name made from the code. We do not have the code and do not open the records. Sending from a crew phone needs that phone’s iCloud sign-in.

The code is the only key, and it is short enough to type. Someone with technical skill who obtained one of these records could try every possible code until one opened it, and that code would open the handover’s photos too. Give the code only to your crew, and stop sharing when the walk is done.

How it ends: Stop sharing takes down what your phone put up, if your phone has a signal at the time. Deleting a handover does not stop its sharing, so stop sharing first. A crew phone takes its own photos off iCloud when its user clears them after they have arrived, and all of its records when its user leaves the handover, if that phone has a signal; stopping sharing on your phone does not take them down, so ask your crew to leave the handover. A photo that arrives is kept on your phone only if its bytes match the fingerprint its sender gave it.

Expenses shared with Ship’s Purse and Logbook

The cost of refueling or filling the water tanks at the handback can pass between Handover and our apps Rainbow’s End Ship’s Purse and Rainbow’s End Logbook, so it is entered once. Each cost travels as a small file, a “card”: its category, amount, date, who was paid, where, a note, the boat’s name and the voyage’s dates, and any receipt picture that came with it.

On one device, a card goes into a folder that only our own apps can read, on that device. To another person’s phone, it goes as a file through the system’s share sheet (AirDrop, Messages, Files), to wherever you send it. Neither way uses a server of ours.

Nothing is sent by itself, and a card that arrives is only offered: nothing is added to the handover until you tap Add. To show the send buttons only when those apps are installed, Handover asks the phone whether they are there; the answer stays on the phone.

What the app does not do

Rainbow’s End Handover contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party data-collection software. It has no background modes. It does not read your contacts or anything else on your device. It works offline, because a boat is often out of signal; only crew photographers need a connection, and their photos wait on the crew phone until there is one.

Purchases

Buying the app is handled entirely by the App Store. We receive no payment details, no card numbers, and no personal information from your purchase.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever changes any of the above, this policy will be updated first, with the effective date above revised, and the change described here.
