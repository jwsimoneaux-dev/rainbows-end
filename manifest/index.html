Rainbow's End Manifest

The book of your boat: what the Coast Guard requires aboard, where it's stowed, the briefing your guests hear, the log of where she's been, and the schedule that keeps her fit. All of it derived from your vessel. All of it on your device.

What it does

  • Boat — her manifest: papers, dimensions, engines, tanks, heads, systems, and the whole fleet — the dinghy and a week's charter each keep their own complete book. Every other page derives from this one, and the book exports to a file you control.
  • Gear — the federal safety-equipment checklist for your boat's length, propulsion, fuel, and waters, in current terminology with citations — plus what prudence adds, down to the ditch bag. Check each item aboard, record where it lives, and set the maximum persons she's equipped for.
  • Briefing — a passenger safety walkthrough generated from your gear map: where things live, a MAYDAY card with your boat's name and MMSI filled in, the crew-overboard drill — shout, point, throw, press — and the house rules, from water discipline to life jackets, tethers, and jacklines at night, offshore, and in heavy weather.
  • Log — the daily cruising log: legs and lay days, the crew actually aboard, voyages that fold into reviewable summaries, a search that finds any day by place, journal, or date, and honest fuel and water estimates that ask to be calibrated before they claim anything.
  • Maintenance — seeded intervals filtered to your systems, hard document expirations, a 60-day lookahead of what needs the skipper's attention, and three ledgers — Fuel, Work, and Ownership — tallying what she costs by month, year, and category.

Manifest checks against the federal minimums; state law can add to them. It prepares you for — and is no substitute for — the free Coast Guard Auxiliary Vessel Safety Check.

Now in beta

Rainbow's End Manifest is on TestFlight, headed for the App Store. It's fully offline — no account, no server, nothing leaves your device.
