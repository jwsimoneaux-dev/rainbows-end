Rainbow’s End Manifest

The book of your boat.

The book of your boat: what the Coast Guard requires aboard, where it's stowed, the briefing your guests hear, the log of where she's been, and the schedule that keeps her fit. All of it derived from your vessel. All of it on your device.

In beta on TestFlight · headed for the App Store

What it does

  • The bookshe reads like a leather-bound ship's book: a cover with her name, paper pages that turn, leather tabs on a wide screen and a contents page in the hand, and the day's quick entries one tap from where it opens. The Editor holds every full form; The Book always brings you home.
  • Her papersthe manifest everything derives from, across six book pages: papers, owners and USCG licenses, dimensions, engines with their hour-meter readings and running hour totals, systems from the water maker to the propane locker, power from shore cord to solar, tanks, batteries — and the miles under her keel, kept for life. The whole fleet, each boat her own complete book, exported to a file you control.
  • Before you goa predeparture checklist drawn from her manifest, with the fuel and water estimates and the flare dates printed on the lines: the blower on a gasoline inboard, the rig on a sailboat, the paddler's short list on a canoe. Checks reset with the date; the log notes the tally.
  • Gearthe federal safety-equipment checklist for your boat's length, propulsion, fuel, and waters, in current terminology with the citation printed under every item — plus what prudence adds, from the ditch bag to the propane shutoff and CO & smoke detectors. And a declarable Inventory for clearing in abroad — firearms, serials, stores, foodstuffs, animals — shared with an official as plain text.
  • Briefinga passenger safety walkthrough generated from your gear map: where things live, a MAYDAY card with your boat's name and MMSI filled in, the crew-overboard drill — shout, point, throw, press — and the house rules, from water discipline to life jackets, tethers, and jacklines at night, offshore, and in heavy weather.
  • Logthe daily cruising log: legs and lay days, the persons actually aboard, voyages started and ended right on the paper with their nautical miles and engine hours totaled, a search that finds any day by place, journal, or date, and honest fuel and water estimates that ask to be calibrated before they claim anything — a powerboat prices her day by hours at trawl, fast cruise, and wide open, against her own three rates.
  • Maintenanceseeded intervals filtered to your systems, hard document expirations, and a 60-day lookahead of what needs the skipper's attention. Engine and generator items watch the running hour meters too: oil due at whichever comes first, the months or the hours. Three ledgers — Fuel, Work, and Ownership — tally what she costs by month, year, and category.
  • iCloud syncoptional, off until you say so: one switch keeps the whole fleet the same on every device signed into your Apple ID, through your own private iCloud. No account with us, no server of ours, unreadable by anyone but you.

Manifest checks against the federal minimums; state law can add to them. It prepares you for — and is no substitute for — the free Coast Guard Auxiliary Vessel Safety Check.

Now in beta

Rainbow's End Manifest is on TestFlight, headed for the App Store. It works fully offline — no account, no server of ours; nothing leaves your device unless you switch on iCloud sync, and then it travels only through your own iCloud.
