Rainbow's End Manifest — Support

Answers to the questions that come up, in the order they usually come up. Anything not covered here: rainbow@svrainbow.net.

Getting started

What does Rainbow's End Manifest actually do?

It keeps the whole book of your boat in one place. From your vessel's particulars it derives the U.S. Coast Guard safety-equipment checklist for your boat — the right items, current terminology, correct citations — and lets you record where each piece is stowed. From that, it generates a passenger safety briefing for your boat, not a generic one. It carries a daily cruising log with voyages, engine hours, and fuel and water estimates, and a maintenance schedule filtered to the systems you actually have. It runs entirely offline.

What should I set up first?

The Boat tab, top to bottom: her name and papers, dimensions (LOA drives the equipment class), propulsion and fuel, heads, generator and A/C, tanks with capacities, and burn rates. Ten minutes once, and every other tab reshapes itself around your answers — change LOA or add a gasoline generator and watch the Gear list redraw.

The checklist

Is the Gear list the law?

The federal items are the federal minimums — 33 CFR and 46 CFR carriage requirements for recreational vessels, each with its citation. Two honest caveats, stated in the app as well: state law can add requirements on its own waters, and a checklist is preparation for — never a substitute for — the free Coast Guard Auxiliary Vessel Safety Check. Pass one and she wears the decal.

Why does the app say "wearable life jacket" and "5-B" instead of "Type II" and "B-I"?

Because the regulations changed. Since 2014 the rules classify PFDs as wearable or throwable, and new labels use performance levels (Level 70 is the everyday jacket). Since April 2022 extinguishers count by UL rating — 5-B and 20-B — and disposable bottles expire 12 years from their stamped manufacture date. Old gear with old labels stays legal while serviceable; the app teaches the current words.

Why did an item appear or disappear from my list?

The list is derived, not fixed. Length class, propulsion, fuel, coastal versus inland waters, heads, a fixed fire system, even a gasoline generator on a diesel boat — each changes what the law requires. If something moved, something on your Boat tab changed.

The briefing

Why does the briefing show "location not recorded"?

The briefing reads from the Gear tab. Check an item aboard and enter where it's stowed — "port cockpit locker" — and the briefing's "Where things live" section says exactly that to your guests. The MAYDAY card fills in your boat's name, call sign, and MMSI from the Boat tab.

The log

What's a voyage? What's a lay day?

Start a voyage, name it, and every entry — under-way legs and lay days alike — collects under it until you end it. A lay day is a day aboard without getting under way (at anchor, in a marina away from home): it keeps the water estimate honest and can log generator hours. Past voyages sit collapsed with their totals — days, legs, miles, engine and sail hours — and expand for review.

How does the fuel estimate work — and how much should I trust it?

It's arithmetic, and it says so. Your burn rate times logged engine hours draws each tank down; fills add; a fill marked topped off — or a measured amount entered from the dipstick or gauge — calibrates the estimate, and the math continues from there. Until a tank has been topped off or measured once, the app says "unknown" rather than guessing. The gauge always outranks the arithmetic. Fresh water works the same way, boat-wide, drawn down per logged day at your per-person rate unless an entry records its own figure.

Maintenance

Where do the intervals come from?

The mainstream conventions — engine-manufacturer schedules, marine-industry practice, and the verified document cycles (USCG documentation renewal, FCC ship station license, NOAA EPIRB registration, flare and extinguisher expirations). Items marked Hard date are legal or safety expirations; past the date, the gear doesn't count. Everything else is a service interval, and your owner's manuals always win.

Why does my boat's maintenance list differ from my friend's?

Same reason as the Gear list: it's derived. Twin engines get port and starboard lines; each Racor-type separator gets its own line; a generator, air conditioning, a sea chest, a composting head each bring their own items; sailboats get the rig category and powerboats don't.

Housekeeping

Where is my data? Is there an account?

No account, no server, no sync. Everything lives on your device and nothing is ever transmitted — see the privacy policy, which is short because there is nothing to disclose. Deleting the app deletes your data.

What happens if I lose or replace my phone?

Two safety nets. First, an iCloud Backup (or an encrypted computer backup) of your device includes the app's data like any other app's — restore a new phone from backup, or migrate with Apple's Quick Start, and the whole book comes with it. Second, Export the book on the Boat page writes everything — every boat, her gear, dates, and log — to one file you control: save it anywhere, and Import a book brings it back. A phone lost with neither a backup nor an export takes its data with it, so export after a good voyage the way you'd photograph a paper logbook.

Does it sync between my iPhone and iPad?

Not automatically — each device keeps its own book, because the app has no account and no server by design (iCloud Backup restores a device; it never merges two live ones). To move the book across, use Export the book on one device and AirDrop the file to the other, then Import — replacing that device's fleet or adding the boats alongside. True background sync through your own iCloud is under consideration for a future version; if it comes, the privacy policy will say so first, plainly.

Does it work with Rainbow's End Anchoring?

They're siblings from the same shop and share the family look, but each stands alone. Anchoring sizes and manages your ground tackle; Manifest keeps the book of the boat. The Gear tab will happily remind you that an anchor is not federally required equipment — and that prudence disagrees.
