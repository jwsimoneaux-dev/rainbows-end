Rainbow's End Galley

Provisioning for sailors, from a day sail to an ocean passage: plan every meal of the voyage on a calendar, mark what's eaten ashore, and the calendar becomes the grocery list. All of it arithmetic you can check. All of it on your device.

What it does

  • Voyage — dates, crew, and the charter industry's own provisioning patterns: full provisioning (every meal aboard) or split (breakfasts and lunches aboard, most dinners ashore — the way charter weeks actually run). Say who drinks what — some want beer, some wine, some neither — which food allergies are aboard, and whether the ship's tanks are drinking water or only showers and dishes. Voyages save forever; last year's charter duplicates into next year's starting point.
  • Calendar — every day carries breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks, and each meal says whether it's eaten at anchor or underway. Tap a meal to plan it: choose from the galley, take a suggestion, send dinner ashore with a note, or trim a travel day. Suggestions are honest arithmetic, not a server: fresh meals land early in the voyage, cans and dry goods late, and a meal underway leans to one pot a cook can manage in a seaway. A meal that won't keep to its day, or that contains a crew allergy, is flagged — with who can't eat it and an alternative, never struck from the menu.
  • Galley — a library of more than forty meals, each carrying per-person quantities, stowage, how long its freshest ingredient keeps, and — for every meal that is more than assembly — the cooking: a few lines for a two-burner galley, one pot where it can be, what to prep at the dock, what needs calm. Your own meals join it permanently with the same math. What's already aboard lives here too — the spice rack, the cans in the bilge — and the beverage rates, per drinker per day, with spirits by a cruiser's rule of thumb.
  • List — the whole voyage becomes one grocery list: every meal's ingredients times the crew aboard that day, merged across meals, minus what's already aboard, rounded up to real packs (you can't buy 13 eggs, so it buys 18). Sorted by aisle, with the invisible half of provisioning appended — cooking oil, paper towels, the propane check. Adjust any quantity, strike a line, add what the meals never knew about; tap items into the cart as you shop; print the list and the calendar — with the cooking for every meal planned — as PDFs for the crew, or export them as spreadsheets.
  • Entirely offline — no account, no server of ours, no signal needed. Nothing you enter ever leaves your device unless you print or export it yourself, through the share sheet.

Keeping windows and water rates are estimates from cruising practice, labeled as estimates. An aid to the galley, not a substitute for the skipper's judgment.
