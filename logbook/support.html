Rainbow’s End Logbook — Support

Answers to the questions that come up, in the order they usually come up. Anything not covered here: rainbow@svrainbow.net.

Getting started

What does Rainbow’s End Logbook actually do?

It keeps one voyage as a book: the boat, the dates and who was aboard on the cover, then a day at a time inside — where you left, where you arrived or where she lay, a note, up to six pictures, a position if you marked one — beside a small ledger of what the voyage cost. At the end, Share makes a PDF of the whole book. It works entirely offline.

Is it for a charter or for my own boat?

Both, on the same screens. A logbook is one voyage. When she is chartered, switch on She’s chartered and note the company and the base. When she is your own, leave that off; her name is remembered for the next logbook, so the second voyage starts with it filled in.

Where is the sample, and can I change it?

On the Logbooks screen under Sample: a weekend on Galveston Bay aboard the ketch Rainbow, with two passages, a lay day, three pictures and a small ledger. The sample itself is read-only, so it stays a worked example. Copy the sample as a new logbook makes a copy of your own to change or delete.

The days

What is the difference between a passage and a lay day?

A passage is a day she moved: where you left, where you arrived, and the nautical miles if you know them. A lay day is a day she stayed — at anchor, on a mooring, in a slip — and needs only the place. A day that both arrived and stayed is one passage; the next morning at the same place is a lay day. Two passages on one date are two days in the book.

Do I have to enter the miles?

No. Miles are optional and are yours to enter, from the chart plotter or your own reckoning. The voyage’s total counts only the passages that have miles, and says so — “3 of 5 passages” — rather than pretending the blank ones were zero.

What does Mark position do?

It reads the phone’s GPS once, at your tap, and writes the position into that day as degrees and decimal minutes, the way a chart reads it. On a passage each place has its own Mark position; a lay day has one. Tap it at the anchorage, not underway, if you want the spot you slept. Clear removes it. Nothing is read in the background and nothing is sent anywhere.

It said “No GPS fix.”

The phone needs a view of the sky, and the first fix can take up to half a minute offshore. Try again on deck. If Location is off for the app, the phone’s Settings will turn it on; Logbook asks for it only when in use.

Which day does a new day get?

The first date of the voyage not yet written. When every date has a day, the next one is the day after the last. Change the date on the day itself if you are writing out of order.

Pictures

How many pictures, and where do they go?

Up to six a day, from your photo library or the camera, each with a caption if you like. Each one is copied into the app at 1,200 pixels on its long side, with a small thumbnail, and kept in the app’s own folder on this device. Your original in the photo library is untouched. The About page says how much space the pictures are taking.

Can I delete a picture, or share one?

Open it from the day’s strip. The viewer swipes between the day’s pictures, takes a caption, shares the one you are looking at through the share sheet, and deletes it on a second tap. Deleting a day deletes its pictures; deleting a logbook deletes them all.

Will my pictures appear on my other device?

Not in this version. The logbook and its pictures live on the device that wrote them. Sharing the PDF is the way to carry the whole book somewhere else.

The ledger

What are the seven categories?

Provisions, meals ashore, fuel, fees and permits, transport ashore, moorings and transient dockage, and other. The charter fee and the airfare were paid long before you stepped aboard; leave them out, or put them under Other if you want the whole holiday in one figure.

Why can a line have no amount?

A free anchorage is worth remembering. A line with no amount is in the ledger and out of every sum; the Expenses screen counts them separately.

Is this a shared kitty?

No. The ledger is your own record of what the voyage cost. It has no people, no shares and no settlement.

What is “a day” in the per-day figure?

The total over the days aboard: first day to last, inclusive. A weekend of three days with $300 spent reads $100 a day, whether or not every day has a line.

Sharing the book

How do I give the crew the logbook?

Share, then Make the PDF logbook. The app builds it on your phone — a cover with the boat, the dates and the crew; a page for each day with its notes and pictures; the ledger with its donut — and hands it to the share sheet. Mail it, AirDrop it, text it or drop it in Files, as you like. Nothing leaves the phone until you send it.

The PDF is big.

Pictures print at the size they are kept, so a week with six pictures a day makes a file of a few tens of megabytes: fine for AirDrop, Mail and Files, sometimes too big for a text message. Fewer pictures on the day means a smaller book.

Can the crew follow along during the voyage?

Not in this version; there is no viewer app and no sharing by code. The book is the skipper’s, and everyone gets it as a PDF when it is done.

Closing and deleting

What does closing a logbook do?

It moves the voyage to Past voyages and makes it read-only, so a stray tap cannot change it. Reopen it from the Logbook screen to change something. A closed logbook can still be shared.

How do I delete a day, or a whole logbook?

At the foot of the day, Delete this day, tapped twice. On the Logbook screen, under the summary and the donut, Delete this logbook, tapped twice (it is on Details as well); its pictures go with it. Neither can be undone.

What it is not

Where are the engine hours, the tanks, the maintenance?

Not here, on purpose. Logbook is the book of the voyage. The book of the boat — the Coast Guard’s gear list, the log with engine hours and tanks, the maintenance schedule, the watch log — is Rainbow’s End Manifest, from the same company.

Does it work on the iPad?

Yes. In landscape above about 900 points the Logbook, Expenses and Details screens use two panes — the summary and the donut on the left, the days on the right. In portrait, and on the phone, everything is one column with the summary pinned at the top.
